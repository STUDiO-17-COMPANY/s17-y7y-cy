## The Physiology of Screen Time

To understand the solution, we must first understand the physiological friction of prolonged screen use. When you stare at a digital device, your blink rate drops by up to 50%, leading to rapid tear evaporation and chronic dry eye.

Simultaneously, the ciliary muscles inside your eyes are forced to maintain a continuous, microscopic state of contraction to keep the pixels in sharp focus. Over an eight-hour workday, this relentless muscle tension manifests as the headaches, blurred vision, and deep ocular fatigue commonly referred to as computer vision syndrome. Furthermore, the high-energy visible blue light emitted by screens scatters easily, creating visual "noise" that forces your eyes to work even harder to establish contrast.
