## The Story of Light

The history of light is, in essence, the history of vision itself. From the moment of the Big Bang, when photons flooded the chaos, light began its eternal journey. Since then, it has never ceased to serve as our sole witness to the existence of the world.

Light has deeply influenced human thought and, consequently, every civilization—from the Far East and the Arab world to Ancient Greece, Rome, and, of course, the Christian tradition. At the same time, both the arts and the sciences have continually sought to master it. We encounter it in poetry, painting, music, sculpture, theatre, and photography, culminating in the seventh art—cinema—where we experience its ultimate celebration.

Countless scientists have devoted their lives to understanding its nature. Galileo Galilei, Leonardo da Vinci, Isaac Newton, Johannes Kepler, James Clerk Maxwell, Albert Einstein, and many others have largely shaped the way we understand light today. We now recognize its dual nature—both wave and particle—while the quantum revolution has revealed that light holds mysteries that often go beyond common sense.

This understanding has led humanity to tremendous advances in medicine, astronomy, communications, energy, and our everyday lives. After all, you are most likely reading this very article right now thanks to the light emitted by a screen.
